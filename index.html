2026 – now Product owner & lead developer Dongding Tech

MIntel — quoting and order platform for a parts distributor

Dongding is a Chinese semiconductor distributor with offices in Wuhan, Richmond BC and Austin. Quoting a hundred-line BOM used to take a salesperson half a day to two days. MIntel takes the customer's BOM in whatever format it arrives, parses it line by line, matches each part against stock and Chinese-made substitutes, and drafts the quote — sales reviews instead of retypes.

The order side writes the company's rules into software: nothing goes to a customer without a manager's approval, a deposit gates shipping, finance confirms payment before the warehouse moves. There is also a public parts shop with an AI selection assistant that only answers from the product database. I wrote the product spec, built it, shipped fifteen releases between August and October, and presented it to management in September.

  • Product spec
  • BOM parsing & matching
  • Approval workflow
  • AI assistant
  • Next.js

MControl — device cloud for buildings and campuses

An open IoT platform: devices connect over MQTT, are modelled as product → thing model → device, readings land in TimescaleDB, rules raise alerts, and tenants get their own workspaces, roles and API keys. How I run a project

I write things down before anyone opens an editor. Requirements, the swimlane of who does what, the list of systems that will be affected. Most of the expensive problems I've seen came from a change nobody traced through to the next system.

I keep sprints small enough to actually finish, and I'd rather ship a narrow release on time than a wide one late. Stand-ups are for blockers, not status.

I stay close to production. Support tickets are the most honest source of requirements there is, and having fixed things at two in the morning makes me a more careful planner at two in the afternoon.
